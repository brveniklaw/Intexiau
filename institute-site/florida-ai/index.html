For parents / Permission and records

Opting in, the non-AI option, and your child's records

From Rule 6A-1.0957(3)(e)1. and 14., F.A.C. · Last checked Nov 14, 2026 · Draft

Does my child need my permission?

Yes, when your child will use the AI tool directly. Your child's school must offer you an opt-in choice, so a child uses the tool only if you say yes.

Before that, the school sends a notice in plain language. It names the tool, the grades and subjects, how students will interact with it (including whether it gives automated feedback), and how to object to its content. (3)(e)1.a.–e.

If you don't respond to the notice, your child hasn't been opted in.

What happens if I don't opt in?

Your child gets a non-AI alternative that is "substantively comparable in instructional quality and access."

That means the same learning goals and standards, useful feedback, and the same access to the lesson, without the AI tool. The notice has to describe this alternative so you can compare. (3)(e)1.e.

Can I see what my child typed?

Approved tools must keep a record of student interactions for at least 30 days so parents can access it as part of their child's education records.

Ask your child's school how to request the record. Your access to education records comes from federal law (FERPA), section 1002.22(2), Florida Statutes, and Rule 6A-1.0955. (3)(e)14.

This page gives general information about Florida's rule. It is not legal advice and doesn't create an attorney-client relationship. For your situation, contact your school or a lawyer.
