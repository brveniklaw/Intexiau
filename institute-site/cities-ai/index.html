Florida cities and counties

AI rules for your city, and a way forward for the people they touch.

Florida has no statewide law on how cities use AI, so each city writes its own rules. This guide starts with a Florida-ready policy toolkit for city staff, and leads to an AI Innovation Center for the city: a place that helps residents into the new AI economy, helps businesses stay competitive, and draws new talent.

City AI Policy Toolkit

Six documents to adapt for your city, each mapped to the policy areas it covers. Download, edit and have your city attorney review.

Twelve areas a Florida city AI policy should cover

No Florida statute requires a city AI policy. These areas come from the laws that already apply to city work and from Florida League of Cities guidance. Outlined items carry a specific Florida or federal requirement.

Building on what's already out there

The Florida League of Cities AI Hub offers guides and checklists, and the GovAI Coalition shares free policy templates. This toolkit turns that guidance into Florida-ready documents a city can adopt.

For city leaders

From AI policy to a city AI Innovation Center

A policy keeps city hall safe. An Innovation Center puts AI to work for the whole city: a better quality of life, help for people whose jobs are changing, new businesses, established businesses that need to shift to stay competitive, and talent that wants to live where the future is being built.

Four steps, at your city's pace

Step 1: Set the rules

Adopt a city AI policy with the toolkit and the self-assessment, so staff use AI safely and within Florida's public records and Sunshine laws.

Open the city toolkit

Step 2: Train city hall

Staff take short badge trainings, and the commission takes Board-Ready AI, so the city leads by example.

See the staff badgesSee Board-Ready AI

Step 3: Open the courses to residents and businesses

Run a first cohort of AI for Everyone and Workforce AI for residents, and Founder AI for local business owners, at a library, community center or partner space.

Browse the courses

Step 4: Launch the city's AI Innovation Center

Make the cohorts permanent, in a home the city already has, with local partners.

What the Innovation Center does

Residents whose work is changing

AI for Everyone and Workforce AI, then a plan for the next job or business that each person writes themselves, connected to the local career center.

New businesses

Founder AI and a studio where founders use AI to test an idea, find customers and launch with less money and fewer hires.

Businesses that need to shift

The AI transition review and Workforce AI for teams, so local employers stay competitive and bring their staff with them.

Talent

A visible reason for remote workers, founders and graduates to choose your city, Prompt Architecture for those who build, and a place to meet once they arrive.

Quality of life

An AI help desk where residents can ask how to spot a scam, use a city service online, or help a parent with a new tool, plus clearer and faster city services as staff put AI to good use.

Built for cities without an innovation center

Many Florida cities have no innovation center of their own. The Innovation Center doesn't need a new building: it can start at city hall, a library, a college or a partner's space, and grow with what the city's college, workforce board, chamber and small business center already offer.

For residents

Own Your Future: a path into the AI economy

For anyone whose job is changing because of AI, who has lost work, or who wants to move into AI work. You build everyday skills first, then a plan for your next job or business that you write yourself.

Already laid off?

Your local CareerSource career center runs federally funded services for dislocated workers, including job search help and training. Federal guidance now lets that funding cover AI skills. Ask them what you qualify for.

For small businesses

AI transition review for local businesses

Three questions sort out where AI fits in your business: what you hand to AI, how you check its work, and what you tell customers and staff. Work through them, then train your team.

Training for owners and teams

Local help

Florida's Small Business Development Centers offer no-cost advising, and several now run AI workshops. Your county economic development office can point you to the nearest one.

For staff and elected officials

City AI training badges

Short self-paced modules built from the city toolkit. Finish the lessons and the knowledge check, and print your badge.

What a badge is, and isn't

An IntexiaU Institute badge shows that you completed a training. It is not college credit, a professional license or continuing education credit, and it carries no state approval.

Want the full course?

Board-Ready AI is a 30-day IntexiaU Institute course for public and private governing boards, a good next step for commissioners and advisory board members. See Board-Ready AI or browse all courses.

Elected officials and the Sunshine Law

The briefing for elected officials is taken individually. Members shouldn't discuss official business with each other through the course or a shared AI workspace.
